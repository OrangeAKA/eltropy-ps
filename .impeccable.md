## Design Context

### Users
**Primary evaluator:** Saahil Kamath, Head of AI / VP Products at Eltropy. Senior PM-eng who will scan this prototype after reading the BID and ask "did this person actually think about what we ship to a CU."

**Buyer persona:** CEO + CFO of a US credit union ($1B-$6B AUM range), with a CIO signing off on integration. Conservative, regulated, risk-averse, will run this through a compliance officer.

**Builder persona:** Director of Operations / VP Member Experience / Business Process Manager at the CU. Composes workflows from a skill catalog. Sees Mission Control as a workspace, not a one-off demo.

**Beneficiary persona:** Front-office officer (MSR, loan officer, branch teller, collections officer). Lives inside the Copilot pane while talking to a member.

**End beneficiary:** The CU member, who never sees the orchestrator but feels its compression of cycle times.

### Brand Personality
Three words: **calm, engineered, credible.**

NOT: edgy, playful, magical, AI-revolutionary. NOT: enterprise-grade-cliche, dashboard-corporate, blue-chip-bland.

Voice (from the surrounding BID + walkthrough docs): builder talking to builder. Concrete numbers, named regulations, atomic commits. Allergic to hype. The product is "the orchestration layer that lets CUs use AI safely," not "AI-powered everything."

### Aesthetic Direction
**Direction: industrial editorial.** A serious financial operations console with the typographic craft of a trade publication (think American Banker, Credit Union Times) rather than a SaaS dashboard.

References:
- Stripe internal admin (composition, restraint)
- Vanta / Drata compliance dashboards (the audit-log-is-the-product feel)
- Trade journal print typography (sober serif headings, generous leading, hairline rules)
- The Trade Desk console (data density done with care)

Anti-references:
- AI Studio starter templates (purple gradients, card grids, icon-in-circle sections)
- Notion-clone minimalism (too consumer)
- Linear (too design-agency for a CU buyer)
- Bloomberg Terminal (too hostile; we're closer to "editorial" than "trader")
- Glassmorphism, neon-on-dark, gradient text

Theme: **light.** CU compliance officers and ops directors work daytime hours in offices. Light is the truthful context. No dark mode unless we add a real ops-floor reason for it.

### Design Principles
1. **The audit log is the product, not a side effect.** Type, color, and surface choices should reinforce that every action is traceable. Hairline rules over decorative shadows. Tabular numerals everywhere a number can change. Component name and timing visible by default.

2. **Vertical depth beats horizontal breadth.** The product's defensibility comes from the layered specifics (Symitar adapter, Reg E timer, FCRA tags). Type and spacing should let those specifics breathe without breaking the gestalt of "this is a workspace, not a brochure."

3. **Editorial typography, industrial chrome.** Pair a confident, slightly serious body sans with a more characterful display treatment for the wordmark / page titles. Use a refined mono for IDs, amounts, durations. Avoid the reflex-pair (Inter + JetBrains Mono is on the banned list).

4. **Tint everything toward brand.** Teal hue (~190-200 H in OKLCH) carries through neutrals, borders, page surface, hover states. No pure white, no pure black. Even 0.005 chroma is perceptible.

5. **Cards earn their existence.** Each card has to BE the interaction (step, offer, member context). Decorative cards collapse to a 1px keyline rule or a tinted background panel.

### Constraints
- Next.js 16.2 App Router, React 19.2, Tailwind v4, shadcn/ui base.
- Live at https://eltropy-ps.vercel.app, must continue to function across changes.
- Desktop-only by design (BID v1 surface).
- Vercel BotID gates automated traffic, so live screenshot verification is manual.
- ~28 commits already on `main`. New work is incremental atomic commits, no rewrites.

### Differentiation
The one thing someone should remember: **the workspace feels like a trade-journal editorial layout that someone forgot to make pretty**, in a good way. Hairline rules, real typography, tabular numerals, named regulations in the chrome. NOT another teal-on-white SaaS card grid.
